distinguishable piece kinds, lay a literal repeating start, offer a genuine
next-piece choice, accept pointing/watch/matching/end, and collect pieces
without another broad search. Secondary stress: spoken color naming is not
available, younger child can reach, or child selects B/does not respond.
Observable success requires actual material counts/roles/first action, one
extension or bounded rescue, stop/cleanup and untested boundary. A critical
omission fails regardless of score. This is a proxy, not parent/child testing.

Separate implementation brief:
- Default EDITORIAL_JUDGMENT: six intact lightweight owned nonmagnetic blocks,
  three kindA and three kindB, visibly distinguishable shape/type OR color.
  No detachable small parts. Adult checks labels/condition/suitability for
  EVERY reachable child; size alone is not assurance. If fit/attention or
  distinguishability fails, skip. No product recommendation.
- Tabletop row, not walking/stepping path. Adult lays A-B-A-B using two of
  each kind, keeps spares and offers remaining A and B together. Six is a
  bounded choice-set arithmetic, not optimal/safe count. Avoid simultaneously
  changing multiple features that prevent noticing the chosen difference.
- Child chooses next A, places or points for adult, then may point/name the
  repeating AB part. Naming colors or explaining verbally is not required.
  Stop after one extension. Watching or declining is valid.
- If B is chosen, adult points to first AB pair and compares the second;
  offer one supported retry, then end or match two alike with adult.
  Matching is not a completed repeating pattern. No drill, assessment or
  inference about learning/ability from a response.
- Adult controls spares, stops for mouthing/throwing/damaged pieces,
  discomfort or diverted attention, gathers all pieces and puts them away.
