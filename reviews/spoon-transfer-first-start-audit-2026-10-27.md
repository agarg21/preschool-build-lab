## Mechanism And Implementation-Ready Brief

RESEARCH_HYPOTHESIS task: caregiver chooses or declines a dry spoon transfer
for a child around3-5, knows what one object/tool/bowl set means, checks fit
before access, models once, lets the child scoop/tip or point/watch/end, and
collects/stores everything. Secondary stress: younger child can reach,
mouthing/shedding, object does not sit on spoon, or attention shifts.
Required outputs: one named set, adult fit check, shared space/role, first
movement and receiving bowl, failed-scoop/drop/repeat choice, stop/cleanup,
and honest untested limits. Missing critical control fails regardless of score.
This is not a real parent/child or physical test.

Frozen mechanism: Montessori Academy Home Lesson Four two-bowl/spoon/tray/
adult demonstration; SDN preschool pom-pom scoop; large-spoon context from
Toy Foundation. Not jug pouring, tongs, food/feeding therapy or timed racing.
The following material restriction/count/fit trial/roles/stops are
EDITORIAL_JUDGMENT reductions, not an inspected source procedure.

- One intact LARGE owned play pom-pom from an age-labelled play set, one
  intact smooth wide/deep spoon the child can hold comfortably, two stable
  unbreakable bowls and a rimmed tray. No loose craft fluff/filling, shedding
  or damaged pom, small/detachable parts, beans/rice/pasta/coins/beads/marbles/
  magnets, food, water or cork substitutions. No suitable owned play pom?
  Choose another activity, not a purchase or improvised filler.
- Adult checks the set's labels/condition for every child who can reach it;
  size and supervision do NOT make a material non-choking. Skip mouthing,
  uncertain parts or inability to keep unsuitable objects/spares away from
  younger reach while attending to all. Do not test anything in a child's
  mouth, certify a size threshold, or imply the age band overrides fit.
- On a stable low clear table away from edges, put bowls side by side on tray,
  with the single pom in one and the other empty. Before the child starts,
  adult checks spoon/hand/bowl fit by gently scooping and tipping that pom
  into the empty bowl once, without squeezing, stabbing, flicking or launching.
  It must rest freely on the spoon. If it will not transfer, skip rather than
  choose smaller loose filler, force it or claim a successful setup.
- Adult remains beside all reachable children and steadies bowls as needed.
  Invite one scoop: move over the empty bowl and tip inside, or point for
  the adult/watch/decline. Let the child use a comfortable hand; no forced
  grip or hand-over-hand instruction. One try is enough, not a speed/count test.
- If it falls into tray, put spoon down first; adult collects and can replace
  the pom once if the child wants, or end. No chasing floor pieces, compulsory
  completion or repeats. Inability to scoop is a reason for one adult model,
  watching or ending, not a developmental diagnosis.
- End for mouthing/chewing, shedding/damage, throwing, forceful squeezing,
  spoon play toward bodies, lost control, discomfort, a piece leaving the
  tray or diverted attention. Adult collects pom and any loose bits, checks
  table/floor, stores pom/spoon/bowls/tray out of reach and clears the surface.
  Do not leave a tray of pieces available or call cleanup measured.
- Keep title/URL and existing routes. Library and four hub summaries must
  name adult role and the same default; remove2min/lowmess/mediumhelp claims.
  Bestfor should describe trying a scoop/carry/tip, not guaranteed focus,
  hand control or a measured fine-motor improvement.
- Put materials/fit/adult involvement/skip/stop before the first movement.
  Research-backed/not-family-tested visible; sources after runnable answer
  explain source mechanics versus exact editorial default. Time, effort,
  mess, owned fit/strength, understanding, enjoyment, learning, repetition and
  safety outcomes remain UNKNOWN.
