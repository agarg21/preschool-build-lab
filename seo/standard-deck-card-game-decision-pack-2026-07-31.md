## Decision

The standard-deck card-game lane is worth validating with real family use.
The current evidence supports one bounded content candidate, not a generic
roundup:

> A parent-facing chooser for five games that can be played with a regular
> deck: Go Fish, Concentration, a finite short-form War, Old Maid, and simple
> Crazy Eights.

The page would help a parent choose by player count, rank/suit readiness,
pace, conflict risk, setup, adult role, and a clearly labeled rescue variant.
Its main search job would be `card games for kids`; `easy card games for kids`,
`card games for kids with a deck of cards`, age variants, and two-player needs
would be subordinate routes on the same page.

This is a separately gated candidate, not an implementation brief. Do not
create the page until the family confirms that all five games can be run,
records structured observations, and can produce original card-layout
diagrams or photos. Research does not establish that a child understood,
enjoyed, repeated, learned from, safely completed, or was not frustrated by
any game.

Snap and Slapjack are not in the first candidate:

- Snap has a good recognition-and-speed hook, but simultaneous calls,
  disputes, and pace need direct observation.
- Slapjack requires physical hand contact over a shared pile and includes
  mistaken-slap penalties. Secondary rules cannot establish its fit for this
  audience.

The Games category remains strategically supported. No live navigation,
canonical strategy, page, generator, sitemap, indexing state, affiliate state,
or external account changes in this transaction.

1. Demand is not low for the parent job. The two broad phrases are estimated
   at 5,400 and 4,400 monthly US searches.

   that demand is expressed. Its unavailable estimate does not negate the
   broader terms.

   routes. They do not justify separate thin pages now.
4. `Best card games for kids` has low difficulty in the tool, but its
   commercial wording and mixed product SERPs would pull a standard-deck page
   toward products unless the page states its no-purchase boundary clearly.
5. The primary candidate should target the broad informational job while
   making the standard-deck constraint unmistakable in the title, direct
   answer, and chooser.

### SERP gap

The sampled pages are generally strong on breadth and recognizable game names.
They are weaker or inconsistent on the questions that determine whether a
parent can start now:

- Does the child need to recognize ranks, suits, or both?
- How many cards must the child hold?
- Is play calm, fast, physical, or dispute-prone?
- Is there a single loser?
- Can the canonical game run indefinitely or much longer than expected?
- Which one rule set is being used when house rules conflict?
- What smaller setup should a parent try if the full game is too much?
- Is the adjustment source-backed, editorial, or actually observed?

Those fields are a potential KAL advantage. They do not become reliable until
the proposed setups are run and documented.

## Candidate Page Model

This is a planned content model, not authorization to build it.

### Ownership

- One page owns `card games for kids` with an explicit regular-deck boundary.
- `Easy`, age 4-6, two-player, and `with a deck of cards` are sections or
  chooser routes.
- Do not create seven game pages.
- Do not mix product recommendations or affiliate links into the first page.
- Do not promise learning, bonding, screen-time reduction, replay, or fun.

### Every-section plan

| Section | Parent job | Required original value | Evidence gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title, H1, and direct answer | Confirm this is a regular-deck, no-purchase chooser | Exact five-game boundary and who each option fits | Research supports wording; no outcomes |
| Start-here chooser | Pick by players, rank/suit readiness, pace, and conflict tolerance | Original comparison table based on tested setups | Must run all five setups |
| Before the first game | Decode ranks, suits, face cards, hand holding, and house-rule agreement | Original compact card diagram and setup photo | Original visual required |
| Parent boundary and stop/reset | Know the adult role, supervision boundary, physical-contact rule, and when to pause, reset, or stop | Human-reviewed guidance tied to each frozen setup; no promise that the game is safe or frustration-free | Source-backed mechanics plus direct observation and human review; safety outcomes remain `UNKNOWN` |
| Go Fish | Run the asking/collecting loop | Exact reduced-deck rescue, observed hand friction, parent prompt | Structured family observation |
| Concentration | Set up an achievable grid | Original 12-card layout diagram and observed reset flow | Structured family observation |
| Short-form War | Get the reveal hook without an endless game | Exact finite end rule, original layout, observed round length without a universal time promise | Structured family observation |
| Old Maid | Use suspense without an avoidable emotional trap | Neutral framing and observed reverse/standard ending comparison | Structured family observation |
| Simple Crazy Eights | Add suit choice as the stretch option | One frozen rule set, original hand/discard diagram, observed teaching friction | Structured family observation |
| Rescue matrix | Adjust when holding, waiting, matching, suits, speed, or losing is too much | Only rescues actually tried, with source/editorial labels | Observation required; no guarantee |
| What was tested | Tell the truth about evidence | Dates, players, setup, what changed, and limitations | Completed intake; no invented quotes/outcomes |
| Sources and variant note | Prevent house-rule confusion | Bicycle primary rules plus named Pagat variant notes | Current source checks |
| Related KAL routes | Continue by age or activity type | Link only to real, non-overlapping pages | Ownership and link audit |

### Index-worthiness gate

The page would be index-worthy only when it contains:

- one original chooser;
- one frozen rule set for each included game;
- original layout diagrams or photos;
- observed setup and rescue notes for all five games;
- a human-reviewed parent-boundary and stop/reset section that does not claim
  a safety outcome;
- explicit unknowns and limitations;
- no unsupported child, safety, learning, or replay claims.

Without those elements it would duplicate stronger existing rules lists and
should not be published.
