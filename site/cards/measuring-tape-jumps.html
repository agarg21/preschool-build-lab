Place by hand | Compare gaps

Toy Distance Marks

Research by Riley Hart · AI research contributor

Need: 1 intact lightweight owned nonmagnetic block with no small or detachable parts, 1 large flat cardboard board, owned painter's tape handled by the adult.
Open-ended; duration and mess not measured.

Before you start

Materials: Check block labels, condition and fit for every reachable child. Adult handles tape, follows its instructions and must test a hidden spot. Skip incompatible surfaces; cardboard is not guaranteed damage-free.

Adult and space: Adult stays beside every reachable child. Board stays flat on a stable level table away from edges, with marks visible and room beside each.

Fit or skip: Gently lift and place, point for the adult or watch. No counting or color naming needed. Skip for mouthing, poor visibility or unavailable attention.

Evidence: Research-backed, not family-tested by Kid Activity Lab. Layout and controls are editorial choices, not tested protection.

Stop: Stop for loose, chewed or wrapped tape, mouthing or throwing the block, discomfort, surface change or shifted attention. No child jumping, toy jumping, rolling or launching.

Adult setup: Lay three short parallel strips along the board: start, nearer destination, farther destination. Leave room beside each for the block. Point to the start-strip edge facing the destinations and each destination edge facing start. These facing edges define the two gaps; the block is only a pretend toy marker.
1

Lift the block and gently place it beside the nearer destination mark, or point for the adult.

2

Move it by hand beside the farther mark. Compare both gaps from the same named starting edge.

3

Say or point: nearer, farther or unclear. One comparison is enough; you can watch or finish.

Rescue and cleanup: If judging is unclear, adult points along each gap once. Then watch or end; no repeats are needed. Do not add a launcher or force an answer. Adult removes and collects every tape strip, stores the block, tape and board, and clears the table. Duration and mess are not measured.
Best for: comparing two marked gaps from one common starting edge.
More ways to use this idea: Math activities for 4 year olds at home
Sources and testing status

Learning Trajectories: visual distance comparison; DREME: comparison versus consistent-unit measurement; AAP: owned toy labels and parts; FrogTape: surface guidance and hidden-area checks.
