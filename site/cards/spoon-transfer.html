Spoon Transfer

Research by Riley Hart · AI research contributor

Need: 1 intact LARGE owned play pom-pom from an age-labelled play set, 1 smooth wide/deep spoon comfortable to hold, 2 stable unbreakable bowls and 1 rimmed tray.
Open-ended; duration and mess not measured.

Before you start

Materials: Adult checks labels, condition and suitability for every reachable child. No shedding, loose filling, damaged or detachable parts. No craft fluff, food, beans, rice, pasta, coins, beads, marbles, magnets, water or cork substitutes. No suitable owned play pom? Choose another activity.

Adult and fit: Stay beside every reachable child and steady bowls as needed. Try when the child can hold the spoon comfortably and pause on request, or point for you. Skip for mouthing, uncertain parts, younger reach you cannot control or unavailable attention. Size and supervision are not a choking guarantee; do not test materials in a mouth.

Evidence: Research-backed, not family-tested by Kid Activity Lab. This one-pom fit trial and controls are editorial adaptations, not tested protection. Watching or declining is enough; no forced grip, counting or spoken answer.

Stop: End for mouthing, shedding, throwing, force, spoon play toward bodies, lost control, discomfort, anything leaving the tray or shifted adult attention. Adult collects all materials.

Adult setup: Use a stable low clear table away from edges. Set bowls side by side on the tray, one pom in one bowl and the other empty. BEFORE child access, adult gently scoops that pom, carries it above the empty bowl and tips it in once. It must rest freely on the spoon: no squeezing, stabbing, flicking or launching. Failed fit? Skip, not smaller filler. Return the pom to the starting bowl if continuing.
1

Try one scoop from the starting bowl. Use a comfortable hand, or point for the adult to scoop while you watch.

2

Carry the spoon above the empty bowl and tip the pom inside. One try is enough; you can stop without finishing.

3

Dropped in the tray? Put the spoon down. Adult collects the pom and may replace it once if you want, or end. Do not chase pieces or race.

Rescue and cleanup: If scooping is difficult, watch one adult model or end; do not force a grip or use smaller filler. A pom leaving the tray ends the activity. Adult collects the pom and any loose bits, checks table and floor, clears the surface and stores ALL pom, spoon, bowls and tray out of reach. Duration, effort and mess are not measured.
Best for: trying a scoop, carry and tip, or pointing for the adult.
