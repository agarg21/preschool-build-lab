Color Mixing Cups

Research by Riley Hart · AI research contributor

Need: 3 clear nonbreakable cups, water, owned red and yellow food coloring, 1 adult spoon, 1 rimmed tray, 1 towel.
Open-ended; duration and mess not measured.

Before you start

Materials: Use intact clear nonbreakable cups and owned food coloring only as its labels permit. No paint, cleaning dye or other chemicals. Adult handles coloring, caps, spoon and liquids; keep supplies away. No tasting or contact with mouths or eyes.

Adult and space: Use a stable level surface away from edges and walking routes. Keep the tray beyond every child's reach but visible. Stay beside every reachable child and control all materials throughout; this is not independent play.

Fit or skip: Try only when every child can watch with hands away and respond to a stop cue. Pointing or describing is optional. Skip for mouthing, reaching, unwanted color play, unsuitable labels or personal constraints, unclear source colors or unavailable adult attention.

Evidence: Research-backed, not family-tested by Kid Activity Lab.

Stop: End and collect if a child reaches for materials, mouths them, liquid spills, anyone is uncomfortable or adult attention shifts.

Adult setup: Away from children, prepare a little red-tinted water in one cup and yellow-tinted water in another, following the coloring labels. Do not fill cups to the top. Store coloring and caps away. Put both cups and a third empty receiving cup on the rimmed tray, with the towel ready. Check the source colors look distinct and the receiver has room for two small spoonfuls; otherwise skip.
1

Look at the two source cups. Point to red or yellow if you want.

2

Watch the adult put one small spoonful of yellow water, then one of red, into the empty receiving cup and gently mix once.

3

Look at the mixture beside the source cups. Say or point: different, the same or unclear. Naming a color is optional; finish whenever you want.

Rescue and cleanup: If the change is unclear or watching is unwanted, end. Do not keep adding dye or pour full cups together to force a result. Adult immediately empties and rinses all three cups and the tray at a sink out of children's reach, dries the work area, and stores the cups, spoon and supplies. Leave no accessible standing water. Duration, mess and staining have not been measured.
Best for: noticing whether two prepared colors look different after an adult mixes them.
