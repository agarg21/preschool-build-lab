Original activity guide

Original Age-4 STEM Test Pack

Research by Riley Hart · AI research contributor

Choose a car roll, a toy bridge, a shadow, a tower or an empty foil boat. Each has an adult setup and a short question to explore together.

Not family-tested by Kid Activity Lab. Timing, engagement and physical outcomes are unknown.

  • Ramp Detective — toy car, low ramp and floor space; dry.
  • Bridge Rescue — paper, two low books and one gently placed object; dry.
  • Shadow Builder — adult-controlled flashlight and a clear wall; dry.
  • Windproof Tower — low blocks and a paper fan; dry.
  • Empty Foil Boat — adult-controlled water tray; no cargo.
Choose or skip: Age four is an editorial starting point, not a suitability test. Follow each toy's age label and instructions; do not lower its age requirement for a younger sibling. Check condition and avoid small or detachable parts. Size alone does not establish fit. If a child mouths materials, or you cannot keep unsuitable pieces out of a younger sibling's reach while attending to everyone, skip the setup.

Plan the whole attempt: Setup, play and cleanup times have not been measured. If you need a guaranteed ten-minute finish, these activities cannot promise it. An adult sets up, stays involved and may handle materials while the child points or watches. Stop for mouthing, damaged pieces, frustration or loss of attention. Gather and store materials before leaving; the boat has additional water cleanup below.

1 · ramps and force

Ramp Detective

A toy-car test where the child investigates which surface makes a car roll farthest.

Check materials, sibling fit and stop rules before handling.

TimeNot measured; allow for setup and cleanup.
Adult jobMake one low ramp and mark where the car stops with tape, socks, or blocks.
Stop afterTry two or three roads, then let it become car play.

Setup

  • Books or couch cushion for a low ramp
  • Flat cardboard or tray
  • Toy car
  • Towel, paper, or a placemat for surface changes

Read-aloud kid steps

  1. Roll the car down the plain ramp.
  2. Put one new road, like the towel or paper, on the ramp.
  3. Guess: will it go far, stop short, or get stuck?
  4. Roll once and mark where it stops.
Say this Which road will make the car go farthest: plain cardboard, towel, or placemat?
Rescue line If this turns into car play, do one last roll and say, "Let's mark this finish spot, then the cars can play."
Safety Keep the ramp low. No standing on books, trays, or couch cushions.
Watch for Does your child change one thing at a time, or do they turn it into free car play? Both are useful notes after one real roll.
Make it easier Use only two surfaces: plain cardboard and towel.
Make it harder Add tape marks and ask which road made the car stop farthest from the ramp.
Optional parent note

Notice: minutes engaged, favorite surface, whether the child made a prediction, and whether marking the stop spot made sense.

2 · engineering

Bridge Rescue

Can a paper bridge hold one object over a pretend river? Start flat, then compare one fold change.

Check materials, sibling fit and stop rules before handling.

TimeNot measured; allow for setup and cleanup.
Adult jobPut two broad closed books flat on the floor or a low table, with a gap shorter than the paper.
Stop afterOne flat try, then an optional folded comparison. Ending after the first try is fine.

Setup

  • One sheet of paper
  • Two low closed books
  • One large lightweight block or soft toy; check suitability for every child who can reach it

Read-aloud kid steps

  1. Lay the paper across the gap with both ends resting well on the book covers.
  2. Place the object gently near the middle. Watch whether the paper stays level, bends or slips.
  3. Remove the object before changing the paper or putting the books away.
Say this What do you think the paper will do? You can imagine the gap is a river.
Safety Stay close; use one low book per side and keep fingers out from underneath. Stop if books slide, the object is thrown, paper tears or goes in a mouth, or the setup becomes climbing play.
If the paper falls Remove the object and move the books closer. Check the empty bridge before trying again. If gentle placement is difficult, the adult can place the object while the child watches, or end the activity.
Try one change Follow the Paper Bridge folding steps with the same paper kind, books, gap, object and placement. Compare what happens; neither version is promised to hold. The guide also covers folding help and cleanup.
Optional parent note

If useful, note what the paper did and which single change you tried. No note or second try is required.

3 · light and shapes

Shadow Builder

Adult check: Keep light on wall, not faces. Switch off if beam leaves wall.

A flashlight-and-block activity where the child changes shadow size and shape by moving objects.

Check materials, sibling fit and stop rules before handling.

TimeNot measured; allow for setup and cleanup.
Adult jobDim the room just enough to see the shadow, set the flashlight on a stable surface pointing at a wall, and keep it still.
Stop afterMake one tiny shadow and one giant shadow.

Setup

  • Flashlight
  • One checked large opaque block, cup, or toy animal suitable for every child who can reach it
  • Wall, door, or plain sheet
  • Painter tape for a shadow target, optional

Read-aloud kid steps

  1. Hold the toy in the beam of light.
  2. Move the toy close to the wall. Look at the shadow.
  3. Move the toy close to the light. Look at it grow.
  4. Make the shadow tiny, then giant.
Say this Can you move the toy, not the light, to make its shadow huge?
Safety Adult keeps the light steady on the wall. Stop and switch it off if the beam cannot stay away from faces.
Rescue line If the flashlight becomes the toy, switch it off and park it. Resume only if an adult can relight it and keep it steady on the wall; your child moves the block or watches you move it. Otherwise end.
Watch for Would your child rather watch you move the object, or point to where it should go?
Make it easier Use one block and only ask for big shadow, small shadow.
Make it harder Build a block tower and trace where its shadow lands.
Optional parent note

Notice: whether keeping the light still helped, whether it stayed wall-pointed, and which shadow change the child noticed.

4 · structure and stability

Windproof Tower

Build low and notice: standing, moved, fell or unclear. Nothing has to fall. Research-backed; not family-tested by Kid Activity Lab.

Check materials, sibling fit and stop rules before handling.

Adult check: Follow owned toy labels and check intact lightweight nonmagnetic blocks without small or detachable parts. Adult prepares and controls the folded paper fan and spares. Floor only, away from walking paths; stay beside every reachable child. Move everyone's hands and bodies clear of possible falling pieces before fanning, with room to wave without touching blocks or people. Retrieve only after the fan stops and pieces settle. Place gently with help, point or watch; skip for unsuitable labels, mouthing, limited space or attention, or unwelcome falling or noise. End for loose parts, mouthing, throwing, climbing, fan contact risk, discomfort or shifted attention. Stop fanning sooner if pieces move or fall, or anyone wants to stop. No electric fan, hairdryer, heavy book or mouth blowing; never fan harder, move closer or build taller to force collapse.

TimeNot measured; allow for setup and cleanup.
Adult jobControl the paper fan from one clear position; stop sooner if pieces move or fall.
Stop afterOne fan test is enough; finish whenever wanted.

Setup

  • 6 matching intact lightweight nonmagnetic blocks with no small or detachable parts; follow owned labels
  • 1 adult-prepared folded paper fan

Adult setup: Check a flat three-block base on the floor. Add up to three blocks on top only if steady and wanted; no more than two layers and below the child's chest. If blocks will not sit flat, use a single row or skip fanning. Choose one clear fan position for any repeat; keep spares out of reach. Counts, layout and waves are editorial choices, not calibrated wind or tested protection.

Read-aloud kid steps

  1. Place three blocks side by side. With adult help, add up to three on top only if steady, or watch. Keep the build below the child's chest.
  2. Move hands and bodies clear. Adult alone makes up to three small slow paper-fan waves from the chosen spot, without touching blocks or people. Stop sooner if pieces move or fall, or anyone wants to stop.
  3. Notice or point: standing, moved, fell or unclear. Wait for the fan to stop and pieces to settle before retrieving. One fan test is enough; you can finish.
Say this What do you notice: standing, moved, fell or unclear?
What counts Standing or unclear is a result, not a reason for stronger wind. A standing or fallen build does not prove wind resistance or safety.
Rescue line We can finish. Adult puts the fan and blocks away and clears the floor promptly, or keeps a flat row only if wanted.
One optional change Only if wanted, make a lower or wider build using the same blocks. Repeat from the same fan position with the same gentle waves; force is not calibrated.
Make it easier Use a single row of the same checked blocks, or point and watch the adult. Skip fanning if falling or noise is unwelcome.
Compare only if wanted Notice the first and changed build without requiring a stronger or taller one. No repeat is necessary.
Optional parent note

Optional note: which build and adult fan position you used, whether it stood, moved, fell or was unclear, and whether you chose to finish. Do not infer learning or wind resistance from this observation alone.

Activity sources: Franklin Institute: block building and hand-held airflow; Kansas Children's Discovery Center: standing and same-distance retesting; Boston Children's Museum: folded-paper fans and no-movement results; AAP: owned toy labels and parts.

5 · water and buoyancy

Empty Foil Boat

Shape an open boat and watch it float, lean or take in water. No cargo or load-carrying challenge. Research-backed; not family-tested by Kid Activity Lab.

Gather: foil, a shallow tray, water and a towel. Leave coins, blocks and other cargo out.

Adult job: set the tray on a stable wipeable surface with the towel underneath, control the water and stay beside the setup throughout. Open the empty-boat steps and fold diagram before starting.

Stop and clear up: mouthing, torn foil, spills, a moving tray, lost attention or wanting to leave end this attempt. Adult gathers foil, empties and stores the tray, and wipes spills before leaving. Even shallow water needs continuous adult supervision.

  1. Raise all four foil edges, pinch corners without cutting and leave a broad flat bottom.
  2. The adult lowers the empty boat and checks it floats clear of the tray bottom and sides.
  3. Say, "Does it stay upright, lean, or let water in?" Ask the adult to lift it out before changing one side, or finish.

If it stalls: the adult lifts and drains the boat onto the towel. Look for a low rim, uneven bottom or tear; reshape one side, replace torn foil or stop. If it cannot float freely in the shallow setup, choose a dry activity instead of a deeper vessel.

Taking part: the adult may do all folding and wet handling while children choose a change or watch. Do not adapt this activity by age alone. Stop if you cannot attend to everyone. Timing, physical results and family outcomes are unknown; sources and limits are in the full empty-boat activity.

Optional questions after trying one
  • Which activity got the fastest yes?
  • Which one lasted the longest?
  • Where did instructions break down?
  • What phrase did your child repeat or invent?
  • Which setup felt annoying enough that you would not repeat it?
